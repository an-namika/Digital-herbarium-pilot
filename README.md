# Digital-herbarium-pilot
Preserving historical plant life of Harris County!

## Extended context:
2026 - 2027 Fondren Fellows pilot of digital herbarium at Rice University - member of TORCH (Texas-Oklahoma Regional Consortium of Herbaria). All biodiversity data will be made available via Symbiota --> GBIF.

## Project Description
Rice is home to a valuable but currently inaccessible herbarium collection, including specimens gathered from Harris County and the Big Thicket Preserve by Professor Emeritus Dr. Paul Harcombe, as well as historical specimens collected by Sandy Ellisk at Herman Brown Park. This project will build a pilot digital herbarium by researching, designing, testing, and implementing a scalable digitization workflow. Through this process, Rice will become connected to a network of national herbaria, historic Harris County botanical life will become accessible to all, and Rice will gain the capacity to expand the herbarium to preserve current campus plant life and document changing ecology.  

## Project Goals
- Establish a repeatable digitization and specimen-handling workflow (color-calibration, scanning, sign-out system) informed by best practices observed at Mercer Arboretum
- Design a metadata/database strategy following Darwin Core standards, integrated with open-source Symbiota data management. 
- Digitize and describe an initial pilot batch of specimens (200), moving alphabetically by family through the Rice herbarium collection.
- Become a registered herbarium, making this biodiversity data accessible to the public and researchers worldwide.
- Connect with digital herbaria throughout Texas and Oklahoma through the Texas-Oklahoma Consortium of Regional Herbaria (TORCH).
- Lay groundwork for expanding digitization to Rice’s broader campus specimen collections, and enabling comparison of historic plant life in Hermann Brown Park (represented by the herbarium samples) to current plant biodiversity via field research.
- Prepare deliverable of an accessible digital herbarium pilot (with at least 200 specimens with complete documentation and metadata) stored jointly in Symbiota and Rice Research Repository or JSTOR, with accompanying ArcGIS StoryMap to communicate geolocation progress. 
- Communicate results of any comparative research on Hermann Brown Park plant life. [Stretch goal: build ArcGIS StoryMap comparing historical and modern plant communities.]
